# Alert evaluation definitions and limits

Status: research assumptions and synthetic reference checks; complete-trajectory held-out evaluation is blocked by the [FD001 usage audit](fd001_alert_holdout_audit.md). These definitions must be frozen with any future eligible dataset before inspecting its alert outcomes. No operational acceptance budget is approved.

The prospective simulated reference event is the supplied last cycle of a genuine complete run-to-failure trajectory. The counting unit is one namespaced engine history with one terminal event. Evaluate every eligible observed cutoff from cycle 30 through the event; no unobserved sensors are reconstructed. The research warning horizon is 45 cycles: detection is an actionable warning/critical state observed within the inclusive interval [event−45, event]. A previously started episode still active in that interval counts as detection. A miss means no such actionable observation; withheld inputs remain in the denominator and follow the retained-concern policy. Warning lead is event minus the earliest actionable observation inside that window; summarize detected engines and disclose missed engines separately.

A false episode is a transition from nonactionable to warning/critical before that detection window, or in a supplied no-event reference history. Warning-to-critical escalation remains one episode. Full run-to-failure data alone cannot provide genuine nonfailure operational controls; early episodes are a research proxy, not established nuisance maintenance events. Count all deduplicated alert-state changes separately, including quality-state transitions. Baseline uses the same predictor, observations and warning/critical thresholds without hysteresis; candidate adds the pre-existing eight-cycle clear margin. Neither is aircraft maintenance guidance.

## Retained synthetic comparison

Reproduced with the existing shared evaluator and `configs/alert_policy.yaml`, using five fixed synthetic histories (four reference events). Both baseline and candidate detected three events, missed one (25%), produced one false episode and had mean warning lead 45 cycles among detected events. State changes fell from 12 to 7. This is a policy reference, not a held-out engine result. The 25% synthetic miss result does not meet the older exploratory 5% target; that target is not an operator-approved acceptance limit.

For sensitivity only, let assumed loss = missed events × missed weight + false episodes × false weight + state changes × change weight. With false weight fixed to one, the retained outcomes imply baseline loss M+1+12C and candidate loss M+1+7C. Thus for assumed M in {1,10,100} and C in {0,0.1,1}, the candidate-minus-baseline differences are respectively {0,−0.5,−5}, independent of M because misses are equal. These dimensionless weights are illustrative research assumptions, never real maintenance costs. No policy or threshold was selected using this calculation, and reduced state changes do not establish safety or alert acceptance.

A future evaluation needs eligible untouched engines, a frozen input/policy manifest, per-engine results, uncertainty on miss/detection rates, warning-time distribution, false episodes per 100 histories and comparison changes under common inputs. Independent operational validation additionally needs authorized real histories, relevant nonfailure exposure and operator definitions/costs. Missing prerequisites remain blocked.
