# Aircraft inspection viewer

The entry route `/fleet` is a spatial inspection workspace. React Three Fiber 9 / Three.js / Drei load a locally bundled GLB on demand. The viewer is an illustrative twin-propeller aircraft, not a validated physical digital twin or a statement of FD001 support for that airframe. Installation anchors and aircraft logistics are synthetic. Unmapped parts are **Not assessed**.

Only component records returned by the API with the selected aircraft identity and `kind=engine` receive engine selection controls. The asset has two engine positions; at most two spatial annotations are displayed. All verified mapped engines remain selectable in the component list, including any beyond the asset's two positions. Selection uses the same callback and URL component identity for annotations and list buttons. Camera pose and component selection are separate state. Switching aircraft reconciles the component selection against that aircraft's IDs; query keys and response identity checks prevent previous component data from becoming its evidence.

Overview and Reset restore the three-quarter camera; Engines moves closer without suggesting an exploded engineering view. Zoom controls constrain distance. There is no automatic rotation or camera animation. Orbit uses pointer input; accessible named buttons supply camera and component actions. No passenger/cabin controls or aircraft dimensions are inferred. The original asset's texture markings are illustrative source material, not product/fleet branding.

The scene uses a neutral token background, soft ground shadow, bounded pixel ratio 1–1.5, on-demand rendering and shared cached geometry/materials. Model clones do not dispose shared resources on selection. Orbit controls invalidate demand frames. A visibility listener suspends rendering in hidden tabs and resumes demand rendering on return; listeners and controls are removed on unmount. Scene exceptions, asset failure, WebGL unavailability/context loss and a 20-second model timeout leave the evidence/list workflow intact. The poster is rendered from the licensed model; no numerical assessment is baked into it. Reload the page to retry a failed renderer/asset.

The initial asset budget is 10 MB compressed with textures no larger than 2K. Median animation-frame interval during a repeatable orbit should be ≤33 ms on the declared demonstration device: this is a proposed performance budget, not validated usability evidence. Measurements, browser/device limitations and screenshots belong in [UI verification](ui_verification.md). No explode or physical degradation animation is implemented because the GLB's separable meshes do not establish engine maintenance geometry or scientific capability.

The registered uncompressed GLB declares no Draco/Meshopt extensions. Its `useGLTF` loader disables those unused decoders so it requires no external decoder URL or WebAssembly CSP relaxation. The fallback poster is a capture of the settled textured model with selection annotations hidden.

The GLB embeds two textures; GLTFLoader reads them through local blob URLs. The development/local and EC2 TLS Nginx CSP therefore include `blob:` in image/connect sources while keeping scripts restricted to self. Capture and mapped-selection regression checks include console errors so blocked texture reads cannot silently pass.
